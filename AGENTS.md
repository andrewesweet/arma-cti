# arma-cti

Personal Arma 3 Capture the Island scenario, developed primarily by autonomous agents with maximal automated testing. Session-based persistent campaign; player as Commander or squad leader.

> **Process status: written 2026-07-30 before first practical use; since exercised by thirty retros.** A rule's earned standing is its own `validated ×N` marker; a rule without one is still a hypothesis until a retro validates it. Propose changes only via `/retro`; never drift silently. Audit trail: `docs/process-log.md`.

## Read first

- `CONTEXT.md` — domain glossary. Use its vocabulary exactly; respect the _Avoid_ lists.
- `docs/adr/` — binding decisions. Flag conflicts explicitly; never silently override.
- `docs/mvp-scope.md` — what is in and out of the MVP.
- `docs/reference/arma-wiki/` — vendored Bohemia wiki, now the whole of it (6,690 pages); the live one is Cloudflare-blocked from here. Check it before experimenting on engine behaviour, and before writing your own version of anything the engine might already do. A day of Phase 0 went to rediscovering one documented sentence; Phase 1 built a code generator around a JSON parser the engine had shipped two versions earlier.
  - Paths are guessable: `commands/setDamage.wiki`, `functions/BIS_fnc_spawnGroup.wiki`, plus `topics/`, `classnames/`, `templates/`. `MANIFEST.json` maps every title to its path and carries the redirect aliases. Read a directory's `INDEX.md` rather than listing it — `commands/` alone holds 2,672 files.
  - Which games a page applies to lives in its `// categories:` header, never in the wikitext: BIKI generates categories from templates, so grepping page source for `Arma 3` finds nothing.
  - Pages tagged only for pre-Arma-3 titles are excluded, so a miss is not proof the wiki lacks the page.

## Seats and profiles

A dispatch names a **seat** — the kind of work — and the seat resolves a **profile**, one opaque `(lane, model, effort)` token, from its own ordered preference list (ADR-0071 ruling 2, replacing the Model roles mapping of 2026-08-04 and its amendment of 2026-08-05). `tools/dispatch.py`'s `SEATS` is the authority and `just dispatch --list` prints it; a paragraph is not a place to keep a pair, and **none is written here or in any surface by hand**. Naming `--profile` stays a way of choosing and never a way around, because a refusal attaches to a `(profile, seat)` pair rather than to the resolution path. An escalation entry is a judgement that the work is harder than the seat's tier, and what resolves through it depends on which resolution is asking. `just dispatch`'s seat resolution never walks it, so a preference list that runs out refuses rather than spending one for you. `tools/arbiter.py`'s walk does the opposite, and both halves hold at once. **What that walk does — its order, its exclusion rungs and what an empty entry gets — is stated in `tools/arbiter.py` and nowhere else, this paragraph included.** Read it there. The copies of that rule were miscounted at all five counts anyone made on #361, so `just check-arbiter` now derives them from `git ls-files` and holds every other surface to a pointer or to a marker carrying its reason (#390).

What each seat is for is the half a registry cannot state:

- **planner** — works out what to do. It neither gates nor lands.
- **implementer** — carries the work out, runs its own gate, lands it. A profile that cannot run its own gate is not an implementer.
- **review** — judges work another profile produced (ADR-0071 ruling 4). Resolution takes that profile as an input and never returns it, prefers a different lane among what is left, and the seat **forces** a read-only permission mode over whatever the caller passed — `--permission-mode` defaults to something writable, so containment here is forced rather than defaulted.
- **recon** — read-only search, triage sweeps, state checks. **A recon claim that decides a routing choice is cited**, so its reader can check the citation rather than trust the summary; no gate reads recon output and nothing downstream ever ranks it.
- **retro** — identifies and researches improvements and **files** them; it lands nothing but its own journal entry in `docs/process-log.md` (ADR-0071 ruling 3 as amended by A4), and each filed item cites the run, issue or commit its finding came from. Finding and implementing are different jobs, and a retro that lands its own findings is the propose-and-approve shape ruling 4 forbids everywhere else. The journal is the single exception because it records what the retro did rather than changing a rule the retro judged, and a retro that filed its own record would leave no durable in-tree trace that it ran; being a self-report is also why it is **reviewed** rather than exempted, so never-alone applies to exactly one artefact per cycle rather than to none.
- **fable** — the #181 shape: a diagnosis whose plausible wrong fix would also have gone green, where no mechanical gate catches the wrong answer. Routing class 4 declares it and `config/escalation-conditions.json`'s fourth condition orders the transfer.
- **orchestrator** — the standing dispatch loop; `docs/agents/orchestration.md` is its operating half.
- **interlocutor** — the human interface: rulings intake, status, observations on existing work, raising issues. **Not a dispatch route** — the human invokes `/interlocutor` in their own session (ADR-0068), and a single invocation buys one turn, so a conversation is set with `/model` and `/effort`.

**Eligibility is not a property of provenance.** ADR-0071 ruling 1 rescinds ADR-0061 decisions 2, 3 and 4 and takes the word *foreign* out of the vocabulary with them: there is no ladder of graded lane authority, and every seat dispatches on every lane. **One** refusal survives that, and it is not a grade: the **orchestrator carve-out** (`claude_only` in `SEATS`), because the seat deciding what everything else does should not move before a tested alternative exists, provisional and ending when the Codex orchestrator backup is built. Routing class 6's keep-on-Claude bridge over the gate paths was the second until ADR-0073 retired it (human instruction 2026-08-18, #406): it exempted the lane that authors nearly every gate change, so the surface most at risk was the one it cleared, and what enforces the conflict-of-interest invariant now is a rung rather than a bar. **A landing whose diff touches one of routing class 6's paths should take its review verdict from a different *lane* than the author's, and where it does not, `just land` says so in its own bytes.** A strong preference, not a rule (human ruling 2026-08-19, #217, carried out by #426 as ADR-0073 Amendment A2, replacing #406's `review_same_lane` refusal and folding in #416's exhaustion rule): the old refusal turned a preference into an indefinite block on completed, gated work whenever authorship had spread across lanes or the one free lane sat inside its off-peak window. Every gate landing now prints exactly one `gate_review=` line naming one of four things — `cross_lane`, the preferred check; `lane_exhausted`, every registered lane an author's; `lane_barred`, a free lane existed and every one was unreachable, each named with the bar (window, breaker, quota); `same_lane_chosen`, a free lane was reachable and a same-lane verdict cleared it anyway. Those last three are different facts and a reader must be able to tell them apart, so the record is mandatory and no flag suppresses it: a downgrade nobody can see is worse than the refusal it replaces. Every cause is derived at landing time from the registry, the records and `dispatch.lane_bar`'s live read — never declared by whoever is landing. Two refusals survive, both for a record that cannot be **computed**: `review_lane_unknown` where a lane cannot be placed in the registry, `gate_class_undetermined` where the policy or the diff could not be read. **`review_same_profile` is untouched and absolute** — the lane rule was strengthening ruling 4, and this ruling relaxes only the strengthening. Take the same-lane review when a cross-lane one is unavailable, not when it is merely inconvenient and not to save a wait measured in minutes. The row's path list is the one authority for what a gate path is, and a gate diff is not exemptible by `config/review-exemptions.json`.

ADR-0061 **Decision 5 survives, and the loss of its neighbours strengthens it**: profiles are not commensurable across lanes, GLM Max and Opus high are not the same quantity and the mapping is not monotonic, so no cross-provider effort scale exists and a level joins a list by being named rather than by an ordering inferred in code. Measured rather than assumed on the z.ai lane: the endpoint ignores `thinking.budget_tokens`, so all five Claude Code effort levels are one configuration there (#225).

Mechanism, and why this section exists: the Agent tool carries no effort parameter, and a subagent silently inherits its dispatcher's model unless overridden — which is how every implementation agent of 2026-08-04 ran on fable and consumed the bulk of a weekly budget. Effort becomes real only through a definition file, so a seat that must enforce a tier on a **subagent** has one in `.claude/agents/`; spawn through it rather than by naming a bare model. Which seats have that surface is decided by `tools/generate_seats.py`'s `AGENT_SURFACES`, and which declare the pair a second time in a skill frontmatter by its `SKILL_SURFACES` — ADR-0068 reaches the interlocutor that way. The generated files under `.claude/agents/` show the result; read them rather than a list here. A seat with no such surface is **dispatch-only**, reached by `just dispatch --seat`, which is a separate process with its own resolved profile rather than a subagent inheriting this session's. Which seats hold a file is a decision and not a consequence of the registry, so a seat gaining one is an explicit edit to the relevant generator tuple. Those files and the interlocutor skill's frontmatter are **generated** from the registry by `tools/generate_seats.py` (`just generate`; #324, ADR-0071 ruling 7), each Claude seat file declaring the first `claude-native` profile in its seat's preference list, because the pair used to be typed wherever a surface wanted it and reached five places for the interlocutor alone. Both surfaces fail **open** — a level that does not exist, or a key that has drifted below the top level, leaves the seat at the session's tier and says nothing — so `just check-seats` asserts the pair is declared and valid (ADR-0068), and `just check`'s generated-file check asserts it is the registry's. **Codex has no seat-definition surface**, so nothing generates there and a subagent a Codex session spawns on its own judgement runs at whatever model that session was started with, with nothing refusing. The harness enumerates `.claude/agents/` once, at session start, so a seat definition written mid-session is not dispatchable in that session (#219 ran both its stand-ins as prompt-prefixed existing seats for exactly this reason); a seat that must persist lands on `main` through this file's sign-off, never as a file in a session's tree. Sessions hand over via `/handoff`.

A lane's enforcement is what it demonstrably runs, proven by the hook-parity suite in pytest — never what its provider claims. The z.ai lane's first live run executed the host's global hooks and never entered this repository's `.claude/hooks/`; that gap is what the suite closes (#225).

## Contract

**Always**: run `just check` + `just unit` after every edit; read the failure bundle before modifying code when one exists.

**Never**: edit an acceptance spec to make it pass; add a sleep, retry, or timeout extension to make a test pass; introduce a bare `random` or bare `sleep` in SQF (seeded PRNG and the `cti_fnc_everyInterval` scheduler adapter only — deliberately not CBA, for the dependency reasoning its header records; #85); call `setGroupOwner` outside `spike/desync-load.sqf` — Squads are never transferred off the server, and `tools/check_sqf_bans.py` enforces it (ADR-0039, human decision on #117; the tool moved out of the shipped addon in ADR-0045); treat `infra_unavailable` as a result.

**Never** `export ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` (or any lane variable) into a shell, a profile, or `~/.claude/settings.json` — lane environments are assembled per invocation by `just dispatch` and nowhere else.

**Never** extend, invent or guess a breaker's wait. A lane reopens at a boundary its provider published, on evidence that it is serving again, or by a human's hand — never on a timer this project chose. (The measured reason: LiteLLM's five-second reactive cooldown against five-hour windows is what disqualified it as this breaker, `docs/research/multi-provider-routing-substrates.md` §3.2.)

The Arma tier shares this machine with the human's play sessions, and WSL2 mirrored networking shares the port space with Windows. The tier may allocate within **[2400, 3000)** (human decision, 2026-08-02, on #47) and must never take 2302–2306. Slot scheme: `2402 + 100×N` per ADR-0028, so slot ports are predictable and Steam's derived +1/+2 stay inside the slot's stride.

## Toolchains

Strictness principle: every tool runs in its strictest practical mode and warnings are errors. A suppression (`# noqa`, `#[allow]`, ignore entry) requires an inline comment justifying it.

- **Python** (daemon + planner in `src/cti_daemon/`, tests in `tests/`): managed by `uv` (locked deps, pinned interpreter; run everything via `uv run`). `ruff` for lint + format; `ty` (Astral) for type checking; `pytest` + `hypothesis`; `coverage.py`; `tools/mutation_smoke.py`, a bespoke bounded mutator over each landing's new test modules rather than a general-purpose engine — `mutmut` and `cosmic-ray` were both measured against it and neither qualified (ADR-0064, #281).
- **Rust** (shim only, `extension/`): toolchain pinned in `rust-toolchain.toml`; `cargo clippy -- -D warnings` and `rustfmt --check` in `just check`; `cargo-xwin` for the Windows `.dll`.
- **SQF** (`addons/`, `missions/`): HEMTT lints are the primary static tier (SQF-VM optional — see docs/research/arma-toolchain.md).
- **Bash** (harness orchestration only): the shell keeps the seams where it is the subject — launching and holding processes, `flock`, environment assembly, signals, `timeout`, flag files between workers. Non-trivial logic — a decision ladder, aggregation, a structured format, arithmetic past a counter — lands in Python under pytest, invoked through a `timeout`-bounded `uv run` and checked at its site; logic already in bash past that line migrates one seam per issue with its tests, never as a big-bang rewrite of the tier that runs the corpus (ADR-0049, #171; inventory on #171. Three conversions landed clean and regression-free — `tools/probe_verdict.py` #171, `tools/host_guard_verdict.py` #161, `tools/pool_merge.py` #185 — each red-first pins into one Python home with every caller failing closed, and #185 routed the class-table residue to #92/#147 at the `CLASS_RANK` comment rather than leaving a parallel table).

Repo hooks (`.claude/hooks/`, wired in `.claude/settings.json`) enforce mechanically: no edits to generated files or acceptance specs (`tests/specs/`), no `git commit --no-verify`, auto-format on edit; and, since #205/#207, no foreground wait of 240 s or more inside a subagent's turn and no `Read` whose window would deliver 40,000+ characters — both fail closed, both denials name their remedy. A denial on a gated surface means propose, don't work around; a token-economy denial means take the remedy it names. Hooks run from each session's own worktree copy of `.claude/hooks/`, so a hook fix landed on `main` governs a session only once that session's worktree rebases: a denial the landed fix should have prevented means a stale copy, not a failed fix (#120's fourth false positive fired exactly this way, during the fix itself).

## Commits, changelog, versioning (ADR-0010)

- Commit messages follow Conventional Commits 1.0.0 (`feat`, `fix`, `docs`, `test`, `refactor`, `perf`, `build`, `chore`, `ci`; optional scope; `BREAKING CHANGE:` footer or `!` for breaking). A `commit-msg` hook (`cog verify`) rejects everything else; if the hook is missing on a fresh clone, run `cog install-hook commit-msg`.
- Any commit with user-visible effect adds a uniquely named Markdown fragment under `changelog.d/` in the same commit (Keep a Changelog 1.1.0 categories: Added/Changed/Deprecated/Removed/Fixed/Security). The changelog is curated for humans — never paste commit logs into it.
- A `changelog.d/` fragment is reviewed as a claim, not as prose: every sentence is checked against the diff exactly as a code comment is, and a fragment claiming more than landed blocks the landing rather than being filed as a follow-up. `scriv collect` folds a fragment verbatim into `CHANGELOG.md` at the next `cog bump`, and `just check`'s fragment leg is content-blind — it verifies that a fragment exists, never that it is true (#429) — so a false sentence there is the one claim in this repository that ships without ever being checked again (human ruling 2026-08-20, #460). The evidence is one session: five fragments claimed more than their diff delivered, each caught by a cross-lane review, while #446's was read sentence by sentence and found accurate — so the rule discriminates rather than merely obstructs. **Nothing here asks `just check` to judge whether a sentence is true**; #429 records that gap deliberately, and a checker that guessed would be #458's token-versus-thing class again. The wording has one home — `tools/brief.py`'s `CHANGELOG_CLAIM_RULE`, rendered into the brief of the implementer who writes the fragment and of the reviewer who judges it, and carried by `docs/review-dispatch.md` as the reviewer's obligation — because three hand-typed copies is #445's finding 3 exactly. Recorded as the human's own decision in ADR-0077.
- Releases: `cog bump --auto` derives the SemVer 2.0.0 bump, runs `scriv collect` to fold and remove every fragment, and tags `vX.Y.Z`. `0.y.z` until MVP scope is fully playable.
- Landing from an agent worktree: write the complete criterion audit as one UTF-8 file outside the worktree, then run `just land --audit-file FILE`. It runs the protocol and refuses by name (#213, #499). It re-gates after the rebase — no flag skips that — and when sandboxing blocks the fast-forward of the main checkout it exits non-zero with `merge_command=` naming the exact command for the orchestrator, because a stale main checkout is where ADR-0042's stale-hook window comes from (#130).

## Working style

- **A turn does not block for five minutes, and which seat it is decides what happens next. An agent that has ended cannot stall** — that is the rule's basis, and it is what survived #218's measurement of the cache arithmetic it used to rest on (human rulings 2026-08-05 on #200, #204 and #218; the retired figures are kept as history in `docs/research/token-efficiency.md` §2). The evidence is the stall record: 226 measured subagent stalls, eleven of them caught only by an external watcher (`docs/agents/recovery.md`), and the #168 agent stalling twice in one evening at the identical point. **In a subagent**, foreseeably long work is dispatched detached and the agent then *ends*: it commits, arms `just watch`, writes a handoff per `docs/agents/handoff.md`, and stops there. The value is entirely in the ending — being woken later is not the cheap half of it. **In the orchestrator**, waiting is what the seat is for, so the orchestrator holds the wait. Foreseeable is not an in-the-moment judgement: it is the measured denial list in `.claude/hooks/deny-subagent-waits.py`, and a gate that overruns an expectation off that list is bad luck, not a breach — carry on. That list reaches the unfiltered regression corpus, so the detached path is the only way a subagent can satisfy the `just regress` row's full corpus, no filter. **A wait that genuinely cannot be decomposed has one sanctioned fallback: dispatch it as a session** — `just dispatch --lane claude-native` — **with `just watch` armed at dispatch and the result read from the ledger** (#218's ruling, decision 1). A dispatched session is detached and nothing wakes anyone when it ends, so a session started and forgotten is not the sanctioned shape: the monitoring burden moves, it does not vanish. Keepalive turns stay unsanctioned, on the same ground rather than on cost — a keepalive exists to hold a turn open across something the agent is not doing, which is the stall shape wearing a timer, and with the fallback in place it is redundant besides. **The exception list is empty**, and grows only at a retro, never by an agent's judgement in the moment — the 226 stalls were all agents that believed staying was fine. None of this is a licence to shorten a gate: the gate finishes sooner running exactly the same assertions. A dispatched session is single-shot: it has no second turn for a background completion or a question. Run awaited work in the foreground; decide routine ambiguities, act, and record the reasoning. If a choice is genuinely the human's, finish the unambiguous part and state exactly what remains and why.
- **Reading a finished verdict is not seat-gated, and what qualifies a reader is the paste rule: quote `just verdict`'s rendered body verbatim; never retype the SHA or the evidence path.** #219's A/B settled it (human ruling 2026-08-05): across 40 scored readings over 8 replayed pools weighted toward reds and not-a-result stops, every arm at every price read every worst class correctly, refused every `infra_unavailable`, and would have landed on no red — and all four failures were the same mechanical act of retyping the tool's output instead of pasting it, once producing a plausible evidence path that resolves to nothing, which is worse than none. Its cleanest comparison is two arms on the same model, effort and tools differing only in their instructions, three failures against none, which is why no fifth seat was ratified. Carry the rule into any briefing that dispatches a verdict reader. The orchestrator reading is not deprecated; `cti-recon` is read-only by definition and may read and report a verdict but never be the seat that lands or dispatches. Whether a dispatch names the right issue, branch and SHA stays with whoever wrote the handoff, not with the reader — a design choice rather than a measured-safe one, and the first thing to re-open if a false gate record ever lands on the wrong issue.
- Deliver what was asked, at the scope intended. Make routine judgement calls yourself; check in only when different readings of the request would lead to materially different work. If the request seems mistaken or a better approach exists, say so in a sentence and continue as asked.
- The gates above are this project's verification, and **two named exceptions are part of it rather than additions to it** (ADR-0071 ruling 4). The first is **never-alone**: no single model instance may both propose a change and produce the review verdict that clears it, so every landing is reviewed by an instance in a different session, except entries on `config/review-exemptions.json` — a list that ships empty, carries each entry's reason beside it, and can never exempt a diff that touches it. The second is the **post-landing review**, retained rather than absorbed because it is the only remaining catch for a real finding an arbiter dismissed. ADR-0071 calls it a seat; the registry holds no such row and none is coming — it is the one `review` seat dispatched again after the landing, so "post-landing" names when the pass runs and never a name `--seat` accepts. Beyond those two, do not add further verification passes, re-checks, or verifier subagents. The rule is kept rather than deleted, because without it "one more reviewer" is an argument available again next week: it was re-based by the human's ruling on #220 from a quality rule to a first-order cost rule — an extra pass is pure generation and generation is the act this plan meters — and #220's measurement has not been overturned.
- Two providers over one diff is **one review pass with two lenses**, not a second pass. The rule above bars adding a *pass*; a second lens adds none — both lenses report into the same review, and a single lens may equally run alone. Provider diversity is the point: one family's blind spots are not another's, which is also why the review seat resolves away from the profile under review and prefers a different lane (ADR-0071 ruling 4). ADR-0061 Decision 3 is withdrawn as a *provenance* rule, and its asymmetry inverts with it: once the verdict gates a landing, a review's false negative is a defect that lands with a clean review attached.
- Delegate to subagents only for sizeable, genuinely independent tracks of work. Do not delegate what you can finish yourself in a handful of tool calls, and never to double-check your own work — never-alone's reviewer is a separately dispatched instance in its own session, never a subagent you spawn, and a subagent you spawn on your own judgement books its outcome to your profile because it cannot be ledgered at all.
- Ground progress claims in tool results from this session: quote failing output, name skipped steps, mark unverified work as unverified.
- An agent watching a run watches it from inside its turn: nothing an agent arms outlives its turn, so a turn that ends "standing by", "monitor armed", or with a gate run parked in the background is a wait nothing will wake. (First instance 2026-08-02; then the #168 agent stalled twice in one evening at the identical point — a background `just unit` finished with nobody to read it, its completed fix committed but unlanded — each stall caught only by the orchestrator's external watcher and cleared only by a prod naming what had finished.) `just watch` (#198) is the sanctioned detachment, not a licence against this: the watcher outlives the turn because its finding lands at the top of a later turn the orchestrator was taking anyway — armed at dispatch, read via `just watch-report`, never waited on from inside a turn.
- Before any work in a worktree, verify it is exclusively yours — clean `git status`, and no foreign uncommitted files appearing during the run — and commit early and often; foreign files mean stop and report, never reset. Standing mitigation while #105 is open: worktree assignment handed two agents one tree five times in one evening (2026-08-02), a routine reset-to-origin at task start is what turns the collision into destroyed work, and the pre-flight prevented a repeat on its first firing.
- Measure before building: a rule proposed on cost or threshold grounds is checked by running the measurement, not settled by intuition.
