### Added

- **Every gate run's wall clock is recorded, and a durable slowdown is said once at the top of an orchestrator's turn (#446).** Between 2026-08-05 and 2026-08-19 the gate roughly doubled and nobody noticed for two weeks: no commit caused it (test count grew 2.43× while the suite's measured work grew 1.23×, a `pytest-xdist` `--dist load` penalty that scales with test count), so no bisect could have stood out — the kind of regression only a standing measurement catches. `just unit` and `just fast` now append one row each to `~/.arma-cti/gate-clock/records.jsonl` — timestamp, recipe, wall seconds, exit status, HEAD, collected test count, load average and foreign gate-process count, so a busy box can be told from a real regression — and `just watch-report` gains a sixth read: one line when the median of the last ten green runs of a recipe exceeds 1.25× its anchor in `tools/gate-clock-anchor.json`. The anchor is the mechanism and moves only by hand, in a diff: a rolling baseline's worst step across the regression window was 1.41× against steady-state noise of 0.85×–1.27×, so it would have normalised to exactly the drift it exists to catch, while the anchor fires on the measured 2026-08-10 shape (1.37×) and stays silent on 08-06 (1.01×) and 08-08 (1.14×) — the derivation, with its day-median table, is `tools/gate_clock.py`'s header. Red runs are recorded with their status but excluded from the comparison (a red suite is faster, and admitting one flatters the median), the report is silent while the Arma tier owns the box, and recording costs one `uv run` start per recipe (~0.3 s against a ~190 s tier) plus one file write at collection time.
