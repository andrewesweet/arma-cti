# The AI Commander against prior art: game AI, academic literature, and published doctrine

**Researched**: 2026-08-01, on issue #48. Three strands read in parallel: Arma-ecosystem and RTS game AI, academic literature, and public US/Russian doctrine.
**Outcome**: five things worth stealing, listed first. **A WEST/EAST behavioural asymmetry is viable at MVP scope** — the parameters that carry the difference are allocation and commitment rules, not weapon systems, and eleven of twenty survive infantry-and-trucks.
**Nothing here changes code, weights, or Orders.** ADR-0014's eight weights and ADR-0027's `ASSAULT_MASS` stand; everything below is a candidate for the sign-off pattern, and the five proposed issues are written out here rather than opened.

Sourcing is tagged throughout: **READ** (fetched and read), **ABSTRACT-ONLY** (snippet or listing only), **UNAVAILABLE** (with the reason). §6 carries the full ledger and the environment failures, which were substantial: `armypubs.army.mil`, `apps.dtic.mil`, `publications.sto.nato.int` and `community.bistudio.com` all refused fetches, and **no UK doctrine was read at all**.

## 1. The steal list

Five items, ranked. Each is a proposed issue, not an issue — triage is the human's and the orchestrator's.

### S1. Response curves and multiplicative scoring, in place of eight summed linear weights

Every serious utility system in the prior art multiplies normalised considerations through authored response curves rather than summing weighted terms, and the reason is exactly our known limit. Lewis (Game AI Pro 3 ch.13, READ) documents the Infinite Axis Utility System: each candidate action is scored by N considerations, each normalised to [0,1] by bookends, remapped by a response curve, and **multiplied** — so a zero on any consideration vetoes the decision outright and the evaluator early-outs, cheap considerations ordered first. Standard curves are published verbatim: linear `(100-d)/100`, `runtime y = 1-x⁶` (decays a decision the longer it has been running), `cooldown y = x⁵` (near-zero until a delay elapses, then spikes), danger `y = 1-(x-1)⁴⁰` (ignore trivial threat, care enormously about moderate threat). Lewis's three consideration classes — mandatory, distinguishing, balance/feel — name our problem precisely: a scorer whose axes are all balance/feel has nothing to gate with, which is what "single-term Assault stretched thin" (#34) is.

> **Proposed issue.** *Curve-and-multiply as an alternative scoring shape for the planner, evaluated offline against recorded campaigns.* Our eight weights sum, so `threat` can only make a Base expensive and never impossible, and every new axis dilutes every existing one — the exact reason ADR-0027 had to add a table beside the scorer rather than a ninth weight. Investigate a multiplicative scorer with per-term response curves over the same terms and the same seeded determinism: `threat` becomes a curve that is flat until a platoon and collapses at a company, `stale_seconds` becomes a freshness consideration rather than a linear multiplier, and a mandatory consideration can veto (a Place we cannot mass for scores zero instead of being barred by a separate rule). This is a scoring-shape change and therefore a gameplay-feel surface: it needs ADR-0014's sign-off pattern, an offline comparison over the existing 200-seed harness showing which decisions move, and an explicit answer to Lewis's compensation-factor problem (multiplying many axes drives every score toward zero) — whose usual fix we could not attribute to a primary source and must verify before adopting.

### S2. An Order as a persistent operation object, with a muster Place and a named abort set

Our Order is a standing instruction naming a Place; it has no lifecycle, no assembly stage, and no vocabulary for ending badly. Civilization V's shipped AI source (Community Patch DLL, `CvAIOperation.h`, READ) is the clearest readable prior art for the alternative: an operation is a persistent multi-turn object with states `RECRUITING_UNITS → GATHERING_FORCES → MOVING_TO_TARGET → AT_TARGET → SUCCESSFUL_FINISH`, a **muster plot distinct from the target plot** (force assembly happens somewhere safe before commitment), and an explicit abort taxonomy: `NO_TARGET, LOST_TARGET, LOST_PATH, NO_MUSTER, NO_UNITS, HALF_STRENGTH, TOO_DANGEROUS, TARGET_ALREADY_CAPTURED, TIMED_OUT, WAR_STATE_CHANGE`. `VerifyOrAdjustTarget()` is the middle option we lack entirely — retarget without aborting. The negative evidence is as strong: ALiVE's TACOM sets `_success = false` on failure and **never reports upward**, so nothing is ever reallocated, and the developers' own TODO admits the commander "always sends 4 randomly selected groups on a task" with a recon→strike→assault→hold phase cycle designed and never built. Vindicta models a muster delay explicitly (`75*log(0.1*eff+1)*(1+2*log(0.0003*dist+1))*0.1 + 2 + random`), so big attacks are inherently slower to launch.

> **Proposed issue.** *Design study: does the Assault want to become an operation object with a muster Place and an abort taxonomy?* ADR-0027's all-or-nothing mass is decided once and then never revisited — a Base that acquires a company after the Squads set off is still assaulted, and a mass that loses half its Squads en route arrives anyway. Every abort reason in the Civ V set is a testable acceptance case we currently cannot express, and `HALF_STRENGTH` / `TOO_DANGEROUS` / `TARGET_ALREADY_CAPTURED` are three failures #35 would have caught. Scope the study to whether a stateful Assault fits behind the existing Command Port (ADR-0012 says new verbs are schema additions) without giving the AI a channel the human Commander lacks, and note that muster-before-commitment is also what makes ADR-0027's "cheapest trip first" detail rule honest — today the mass converges on the target from wherever it stood. This touches the Order surface and is therefore fable-session material, not an implementation ticket.

### S3. A force-ratio threshold derived from our own units, replacing the hand-tuned massing table

`ASSAULT_MASS` maps an echelon band to a Squad count by authored judgement (`team: 1, squad: 2, platoon: 3, company: 4`), tuned on 200 Stratis seeds and flagged for sign-off. Uriarte & Ontañón, *Combat Models for RTS Games* (arXiv:1605.05305, READ pp.2–5) give the closed form that would replace the judgement: under TS-Lanchester², with `α = avgDPF(B,A)/HP̄(A)` and `β = avgDPF(A,B)/HP̄(B)`, side A wins iff `|A₀|/|B₀| > R_α` where **`R_α = √(α/β)`**, with closed forms for battle duration and survivors as free extras. Parameters can be hand-authored from the units' own damage and health as an acceptable start (they say so explicitly, having compared learned DPF against manual values). Doctrine independently supplies the shape rather than the number: ATP 2-01.3 (2019) Table B-15 and FM 6-0 (2014) Table 9-2 both publish 3:1 prepared attack, 2.5:1 hasty, 1:1 flank counterattack, 1:2.5 hasty defence, 1:3 prepared defence — but the Dupuy Institute's history of the rule (READ) shows it is a planning factor for initial dispositions, not a prediction, and that WWII attacks most commonly occurred at 1:1 and succeeded 63% of the time. Read together: take the monotone posture-indexed *table shape* from doctrine and the *thresholds* from our own units.

> **Proposed issue.** *Derive the Assault mass from a force ratio rather than an authored band table.* ADR-0027 chose a table deliberately — dividing observed men by eight would invent the number #28's fog exists to withhold — and any ratio rule must respect that: the ratio's denominator is the band's floor (a company is "25 or more", so plan against 25), not a reconstructed count. The prize is that `R_α` is computed from our own rifle squad's DPF and health, so the table stops being a Stratis-tuned constant and starts being a consequence of the units, surviving Phase 4's vehicles without retuning; the closed-form duration is also the first principled input to a muster or timeout rule (S2). Deliverable is an offline comparison of `R_α`-derived masses against the current four rows on the same 200 seeds, plus an explicit statement of what happens at the band floor versus the band ceiling — ADR-0027's 1.14-point gate is the margin this must not eat.

### S4. Per-class contact decay, and an offline error metric for tuning the fog model

Weber, Mateas & Jhala, *A Particle Model for State Estimation in RTS Games* (AIIDE-11, READ pp.103–106) is the closest thing in the literature to our banded Contacts, and it is ahead of us in two specific ways. First, **decay rate is per unit class, not global**: fitted values give ground attackers a 22.2 s effective lifetime and air 5.8 s, while buildings and workers never decay — because a low-mobility prediction stays true and a high-mobility one does not. Our `stale_seconds = 300.0` is one number for everything, and Phase 4's `posture` field (`foot`, `motorised`, `mechanised`) is exactly the class distinction their result says should drive it. Second, they define a **region-based error metric over precisely our shape of abstraction**: `error(t) = Σ_r |p(r,t) − a(r,t)|` summed over regions, bounded between a null model and a perfect tracker, with parameters fitted offline by Nelder–Mead against recorded replays — a deterministic, seed-free, no-in-world-run tuning harness. Their fourth update step, culling particles inside own vision range, is the one thing we already have: #28's removal rule is observed-absence, and ADR-0027 leans on it ("looking clears the Contact outright").

> **Proposed issue.** *Per-posture Contact decay, and a replay-scored error metric for the fog model.* `stale_seconds` is a single 300 s constant applied to every Contact regardless of what was seen, which is defensible only while `posture` cannot vary (MVP is foot and motorised); the moment Phase 4 lands vehicles it is wrong in the direction that matters, because a motorised Contact is the one most likely to have left. The cheap half is a per-posture decay table with the same structure as `ECHELON_THREAT`. The more valuable half is the metric: an offline harness that scores a fog model against recorded sessions by per-Place threat error, bounded by null and perfect trackers, would let us tune decay — and ADR-0027's age asymmetry — without spending an Arma slot, which is the same argument #45 made for the tier generally. Note the honest limit: their model tracks individual units and ours deliberately cannot, so only the decay-and-error machinery transfers, not the particles.

### S5. A doctrine-derived WEST/EAST parameter pack

The doctrine strand's verdict is that the asymmetry is viable now (§4). Eleven of twenty candidate parameters survive at infantry-and-trucks scope, and the ones a player would feel are allocation and commitment rules that our existing tables can already express: EAST commits a fixed fraction of its force regardless of what is defending and holds a large pre-tasked second echelon it throws at wherever it has already won; WEST sizes its package to the garrison, holds a smaller free reserve it throws wherever it most helps, halts at culmination and digs in, and bypasses anything under a stated size. One caveat is load-bearing: ADR-0027's all-or-nothing commitment forbids a held-back second echelon, which is EAST's single most distinctive behaviour, so the massing table would need a second column (first-echelon Squads, second-echelon Squads) for the asymmetry to read at all.

> **Proposed issue.** *A doctrine parameter set per side, behind a manifest-authored faction profile.* Today both Commanders run identical weights and an identical `ASSAULT_MASS`, so the only thing distinguishing WEST from EAST at the strategic layer is the seed's jitter and the map. §3's P1–P20 table gives a sourced starting set for two profiles; the MVP-scope subset is P1, P2, P3, P4, P5, P6, P7, P8, P10, P11, P14. This is a gameplay balance and feel decision end to end and cannot land without human sign-off; the ticket's deliverable is a profile shape and a playtest brief that asks the one question that matters — *does fighting EAST feel different from fighting WEST, without a briefing telling you so?* Two things must travel with it: every EAST fraction is sourced to ATP 7-100.1 (2024) and is solid, but every EAST distance, rate and ratio comes from FM 100-2-1 (1984), a US Cold War assessment of Soviet practice rather than a Russian document, and must be cited as such; and §3.3's list of unsupported contrasts must be carried into the profile's own comments so a future agent does not encode the myths.

## 3. Doctrine as candidate parameters

### 3.1 What the primary sources actually say, in four findings

**The force-ratio table is real, published, and in neither of the manuals the issue named.** FM 3-90 has never carried it, in any edition reached — 2001, 2013 (as FM 3-90-1), 2019 (ADP 3-90) and 2023 all return empty match sets for planning ratios. It lives in **FM 6-0 (2014) Table 9-2, "Historical minimum planning ratios", p. 9-20** (Delay 1:6, Defend prepared 1:3, Defend hasty 1:2.5, Attack prepared 3:1, Attack hasty 2.5:1, Counterattack flank 1:1) and in **ATP 2-01.3 (2019) Table B-15, p. B-9** (identical but Delay 1:7). The in-doctrine disclaimer is worth as much as the table: FM 6-0 9-104, "Combat power comparisons are provisional at best. Arraying forces is tricky, inexact work."

**The headline attack ratio is ~3:1 on both sides, so there is no asymmetry in the number.** FM 100-2-1 (1984) p. 2-11 gives "an aggregate ratio of combat power of approximately 3:1 is sufficient" with 4–5:1 in personnel at the main attack. The asymmetry is in *where the ratio is manufactured*: WEST states it per mission; the Soviet method narrows frontage until 3:1 exists locally while accepting 2:1 overall — "a guards rifle corps … concentrated 80 to 90 percent of its force into a sector less than one third the width of the zone".

**Echelonment is the strongest genuine asymmetry, and it is one of publication rather than of practice.** ATP 7-100.1 (2024) publishes fractions: first echelon "one-half to two-thirds of the available combat power" (7-22), second echelon "one-half to one-third … **with a specific mission objective as opposed to a reserve's mission**" (7-23), and 3-25 says that assignment is exactly what distinguishes it from a reserve. WEST refuses to publish an equivalent: FM 3-90 (2023) 5-7 gives only "in a hasty attack a reserve can contain up to one third of a force's combat power", 4-11 "the more unknown the enemy situation, the larger the size of the reserve", and ADP 3-90 sizes the reserve on "the level of uncertainty".

**The commitment triggers genuinely differ, and this is what a player would feel.** EAST commits the second echelon onto success: "Second echelon forces are used to reinforce success, not failure" (FM 100-2-1 p. 2-10), normally after the immediate objective is achieved. WEST commits the reserve "to exploit success or prevent failure", whereupon it "normally become[s] or reinforce[s] the echelon's main effort" and a new reserve is reconstituted (FM 3-90 1-94, 1-95). And the two sides end an attack differently: WEST halts at a culminating point and transitions to the defence (ADP 3-90 3-115/3-116); ATP 7-100.1 has no culminating-point concept at all and instead replaces — "Replace units … who have lost combat effectiveness" (6-7), the assault detachment "immediately withdrawn and reconstituted" (7-113).

### 3.3 Where the popular WEST/EAST contrast is *not* supported

This list exists so a future agent does not encode the myths. Each item was checked against the primary text and the negative is a verified empty match set unless stated.

1. **"WEST plans to 3:1, EAST just masses" — false.** Both land at approximately 3:1. The asymmetry is in how the ratio is manufactured (P12), not in the number.
2. **"EAST is rigid and numeric, WEST is flexible" — half true and edition-dependent.** TC 7-100.2 (2011) is as unprescriptive as FM 3-90: its reserve is "entirely situation-dependent" and it carries no first/second echelon attack doctrine at all. The fractions are in ATP 7-100.1 (2024) and are strongest in FM 100-2-1 (1984).
3. **ATP 7-100.1 (2024) publishes no force ratios, no rate of advance, no numeric bypass criterion, no acceptable-loss threshold and no company or platoon frontage** — all verified empty. Every parameter needing those numbers sources to FM 100-2-1, a 1984 US assessment, and must be labelled so.
4. **"Bypass criteria" is a Western concept.** Defined and delegable in FM 3-90 and ADP 3-90; absent as a formal concept from TC 7-100.2; present in ATP 7-100.1 only as guidance handed down from higher.
5. **Reconnaissance push versus pull is not a WEST/EAST axis.** It is WEST-internal, and neither FM 3-90 nor FM 3-98 states a preference between them — both are conditioned on situational understanding. The honest EAST contrast is the *absence* of the distinction, plus attack-from-the-march.
6. **Neither side publishes "halt at N% losses".** WEST culmination is a judgement with named indicators; EAST replaces rather than culminating. P6 is doctrinally *motivated* design, not a doctrinally *specified* threshold.
7. **No UK doctrine was read.** ADP Land Operations was reached as front matter only and JDP 0-01 not at all. Nothing here supports any claim about British doctrine.

Two further honesty notes carried from the strand. The 18:1 "penetration (lead element)" row that circulates with the ratio table rests on a secondary reproduction of ATP 5-0.2 and was **not** verified against a primary. And FM 3-0 (2025) has dropped the decisive-operation/shaping-operation framework entirely, keeping main effort and supporting effort — so a parameter written against the older vocabulary is written against a superseded edition.

### 3.4 Verdict: viable at MVP scope

**Yes, comfortably.** The eleven MVP-scope parameters are allocation and commitment rules, and they express differences a squad-leader player meets directly: EAST arrives with a fixed large package that does not care how many defenders you have and then keeps feeding it, so a reinforced Objective does not deter it and its attacks do not stop; WEST sizes the package to your garrison, leaks past small outposts under a bypass rule, halts at culmination and digs in, and changes its mind when its picture changes. The Phase-4+ half — artillery norms, recon-fire complexes, 50 km forward detachments, frontages in metres — is the decorative half, and losing it costs the asymmetry nothing.

The one structural blocker is ours, not doctrine's: **ADR-0027's all-or-nothing commitment forbids a held-back second echelon**, which is P4, which is EAST's most distinctive behaviour. Either the massing table gains a second column or EAST reads as WEST-with-different-numbers.
